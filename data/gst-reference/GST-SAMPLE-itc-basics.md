# Input Tax Credit (ITC) Basics

## Conditions to claim ITC (Section 16, CGST Act)

A registered person can claim ITC only when ALL of these conditions are satisfied:

1. **Possession of a valid tax invoice or debit note** from a registered supplier (or self-invoice under reverse charge).
2. **Actual receipt of the goods or services** (received by the taxpayer or on their direction).
3. **Tax charged has been paid to the government** by the supplier.
4. **The supplier has filed their GSTR-1**, and the invoice is reflected in the recipient's **GSTR-2B** (Section 16(2)(aa) — since January 1, 2022, no provisional ITC can be claimed outside GSTR-2B; Rule 36(4) provisional-ITC allowance is obsolete).
5. **The recipient has furnished their own GST return** (GSTR-3B).
6. Payment to the supplier must be made **within 180 days** of the invoice date, or the claimed ITC must be reversed (with interest) until payment is made.

## Time limit for claiming ITC

ITC for an invoice/debit note must be claimed before the earlier of:
- The due date for filing the return for September following the end of the financial year, or
- The date of filing the relevant annual return.

## Books vs. GSTR-2B reconciliation

ITC claimed in the taxpayer's books must reconcile with GSTR-2B. Any mismatch must be resolved before claiming — ITC should never be claimed purely from books/invoices if it doesn't appear in GSTR-2B.

## Note on blocked credit
Even when all Section 16 conditions above are met, certain categories of ITC are still disallowed under Section 17(5) — see the separate blocked-credit reference document for that list.

## Source note
Based on CGST Act Section 16 and Section 16(2)(aa) (effective since Jan 1, 2022). Always verify current rules before advising on a specific ITC claim.
